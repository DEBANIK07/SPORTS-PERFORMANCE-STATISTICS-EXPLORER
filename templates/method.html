How improvement is assessed

The goal: tell steady progress apart from a single standout result.

Times are flipped in sign before analysis so that “higher = better” for every event. With at least {{ min_sessions }} sessions, five pieces of evidence are computed with NumPy:

  1. Trend slope — a least-squares line through every session (np.polyfit).
  2. Gains hit rate — the share of session-to-session changes that were improvements.
  3. Rank correlation — Spearman’s ρ between session number and performance (are results ordered?).
  4. Biggest-jump share — how much of all the gains came from the single largest jump, and how large that jump is versus a typical change.
  5. Leave-one-out trend — the slope is recomputed with each session removed. If removing one session wipes out most of the trend, that session was carrying it.

Verdicts

Consistent improvementPositive trend, ≥60% of changes are gains, ρ ≥ 0.7, trend clearly above noise, and no single session is propping it up.
Driven by one resultPositive trend, but one jump is ≥2.5× a typical change and either supplies ≥60% of the gains or its removal halves the trend.
Inconsistent improvementTrending up, but results bounce around too much to call it steady.
No clear improvementFlat trend, or an upward drift smaller than normal session noise.
DecliningTrend going the wrong way and results ordered that way (ρ ≤ −0.5).

A “driven by one result” verdict can mean a one-off great day (performance falls back afterwards) or a genuine breakthrough that held. Check the athlete’s chart: if results stay at the new level, it’s a step change, not a fluke.
